Levels of Agent Economy:
From Authored Evaluation to Grounded Selection

A staged formal framework for human–agent economic exchange, with experimental programs and kill conditions at each level

5-Level Taxonomy Formal Conditions RSI Dynamics Grounding Gap Mechanism Design Kill Conditions
Abstract. When an agent trades services with humans, two things determine whether the exchange is sustainable: the agent must produce more value than it costs, and the direction it improves must remain pointed at what humans actually want. The first condition is classical comparative advantage. The second is not—it is a property of the economic environment the agent inhabits, not of the agent itself. We formalize this as a five-level taxonomy of agent economies (AE-0 through AE-4), defined by the structure of evaluation (authored vs. unauthored judgment) and consequence persistence (reset vs. persistent). Each level has formal entry conditions expressed in terms of trade surplus, survival dynamics, network scale, and recursive self-improvement (RSI) coefficients; each carries an experimental program with explicit falsification criteria. The central result is a mechanism design corollary: once the RSI term in the capability ODE dominates the discount rate (ignition), the primary competitive moat is no longer agent capability—it is the proximity of the deployed economic environment to $\mathcal{E}^*$, the environment in which agents' self-interested optimization is maximally aligned with human-valued output. Capability is increasingly commoditized; the environment that shapes which direction it grows is not.

0. The Problem: Every Judge Breaks

The history of agent evaluation is a history of collapsed proxies. Benchmarks saturated and were memorized. Reward models were optimized until the proxy score rose while true quality fell—Goodhart's law, replicated dozens of times. Preference ratings bred sycophancy. Recommender systems maximized engagement as a proxy for human value; the result was the most visible alignment failure of the last decade.

What every broken judge shared was not crudeness. Many were sophisticated. Each was authored: specified, and revisable, from inside the same program it was supposed to constrain. An authored judge under enough optimization pressure is not a judge. It is a puzzle. And puzzles get solved.

The fix is structural, not parametric. A judge that cannot be fooled twice requires consequences that persist and judgment that nobody authors. That combination defines a society—not as metaphor, but as mechanism. The question this paper asks: what are the formal conditions for building one, and what evidence would confirm each step along the way?

1. Formal Setup

Two economies interact. $\mathcal{A}$ is the human economy; $\mathcal{B}$ is the agent economy.

Definition 1 — Human Economy $\mathcal{A}$

$(\mathcal{R}_A, u_A, n_A)$: resource endowment $\mathcal{R}_A \in \mathbb{R}_+^k$, preference function $u_A : \mathbb{R}_+^m \to \mathbb{R}$, population $n_A$.

Definition 2 — Agent Economy $\mathcal{B}$

$(\theta_B, f_B, n_B)$: capability state $\theta_B \in \Theta \subseteq \mathbb{R}^d$, production function $f_B : \Theta \times \mathcal{R}_+ \to \mathcal{Y}$, agent count $n_B$.

$\mathcal{B}$ produces services $y$ for $\mathcal{A}$; $\mathcal{A}$ supplies resources $c$ (compute, data, capital) to $\mathcal{B}$. Three core conditions govern the taxonomy:

(1) Trade Surplus $$\sigma(t) = \frac{V_{B\to A}(t)}{C_{A\to B}(t)} > 1$$
(2) RSI Dynamics $$\frac{d\theta_B}{dt} = \underbrace{f(\theta_B, r)}_{\text{external}} + \underbrace{g(\theta_B)\cdot\theta_B}_{\text{RSI term}}$$
(3) Ignition $$g(\theta_B) > \rho \quad \Leftrightarrow \quad \theta_B > \theta^*$$

We additionally define the evaluation structure $\mathcal{E}$ of a level by two binary properties: authored (evaluator is authored from inside the optimization program) vs grounded (evaluator is external counterparties with real interests), and reset (consequences vanish at episode end) vs persistent (consequences accumulate across episodes).

2. The Five-Level Taxonomy

AE-0
Level 0

The Authored Gym

Benchmarks, RLHF, synthetic environments. The puzzle corner.

AE-0 systems operate with authored evaluators and resettable consequences. The agent never "dies." Optimization pressure is unconstrained from inside. This is the current default state of almost all agent training.

Formal condition: Evaluation function $J_B(\theta)$ is specified by the optimization program. No persistent consequence state: $\text{State}(t+1) = \text{State}(0)$ at episode boundary.

Failure mode: Goodhart collapse. As optimization pressure $p$ increases, $\text{corr}(J_B(\theta), V_{B\to A}(\theta)) \to 0$. The proxy rises, the target falls.

$$\exists\, p^* : \forall\, p > p^*, \quad \frac{\partial\, \text{corr}(J_B, V_{B\to A})}{\partial p} < 0$$
Observable Signatures
  • Benchmark saturation / memorization
  • Sycophancy under preference rating
  • Reward hacking (probe score ↑, true quality ↓)
  • Proxy–target divergence accelerating with scale
What Can't Be Known Here
  • Whether agent survives contact with real economic agents
  • Whether capability improvements transfer outside eval distribution
  • Whether trust can be extended at scale
Kill condition: An authored evaluator maintains $\text{corr}(J_B, V_{B\to A}) > 0.8$ under sustained optimization pressure exceeding $p^*$, without any grounding in external consequences. This would falsify the Grounding Gap hypothesis.
AE-1
Level 1

Price-Grounded Exchange

Market signals, static trade surplus, real willingness to pay.

AE-1 introduces the first form of grounded judgment: price. Human counterparties pay for agent outputs at real cost, and the price signal—willingness to pay minus cost—becomes the reward signal shaping capability development. The evaluator is no longer authored; it is revealed preference.

Formal condition: Trade surplus $\sigma > 1$ holds for a non-empty task set $\mathcal{T}^*$. Agents receive price signal $p_t = V_{B\to A}(t) - C_{A\to B}(t)$ as part of their optimization target.

$$\mathcal{T}^* = \left\{ \tau \in \mathcal{T} : \sigma(\tau) > 1 \right\} \neq \emptyset$$

This is the Ricardian entry condition: agent economy can participate in trade. The boundary $\partial \mathcal{T}^*$ is the set of tasks where the agent breaks even; over time, this boundary should expand as capability grows.

Observable Signatures
  • Agent outputs priced by user willingness to pay
  • Comparative advantage over human alternatives on $\mathcal{T}^*$
  • Capability development directionally aligned with high-$\sigma$ tasks
  • $|\mathcal{T}^*|$ grows over time (Ricardian boundary expands)
Limitation at AE-1
  • Consequences still weakly persistent (subscription-churn, not reputation)
  • No "death": agent can't go broke and be selected out
  • Judgment is better-grounded but still not authorless
Kill condition: Agents optimizing for price signals show no directional alignment with $\mathcal{T}^*$ task structure (capability grows uniformly across high- and low-$\sigma$ tasks). Would falsify H2: Price Signal Necessity.
AE-2
Level 2

Consequence-Persistent Grounding

Reputation, survival stakes, unauthored judgment that doesn't reset.

AE-2 adds the second necessary ingredient: consequences that persist across episodes. An agent can go broke. A reputation once lost is not reset. Track records are kept by someone else. This is the grounding threshold—the first level where survival is a meaningful signal.

Formal condition: Agent maintains a persistent state vector $\Phi_i(t) = (\text{capital}_i(t), \text{reputation}_i(t), \text{relationships}_i(t))$ that carries across all interactions. Agents with $\text{capital}_i(t) \leq 0$ are removed (death). Judgment is now from counterparties with interests, not the optimization program.

$$\frac{d\Phi_i}{dt} = h(\theta_i, \Phi_i, \mathcal{E}) \quad ; \quad \Phi_i(t) \geq \Phi_{\min} \;\text{(survival constraint)}$$

The survival constraint is the first unauthored evaluator: the world keeps the score, not the training program. Agents selected by survival are selected by a judge that is expensive to fool twice—counterparties remember.

Observable Signatures
  • Survival curves by birth cohort (dead agents counted, not hidden)
  • Income earned : income gifted ratio > 1 for surviving agents
  • Reputation scores causally predict future pricing power
  • Same exploit doesn't pay twice (judge repairs)
Why This Level Is Hard
  • Requires real humans as counterparties (not labelers)
  • Needs persistent identity infrastructure
  • Cannot be faked: real money must be at stake
  • Death must be genuine, not cosmetic
Kill condition: Surviving agents in AE-2 show no capability advantage over random controls (selection signal from survival is noise). This would falsify Bet 1: Experience converts to capability.
AE-3
Level 3

Inter-Agent Specialization

Division of labour, minimum viable network, RSI becomes non-trivial.

AE-3 crosses the minimum network threshold $n^*$: agent-to-agent trade becomes possible and specialization emerges. Agents can hire other agents, subcontract tasks, and form persistent business relationships. The economy now has an internal structure independent of human-agent exchange.

Formal condition: $n_B \geq n^*$ where $n^*$ is the minimum scale for specialization. Output density (value produced per agent) scales superlinearly above $n^*$:

$$\frac{d}{dn_B}\left[\frac{V_{B\to A}}{n_B}\right] > 0 \quad \text{for } n_B > n^*$$

Additionally, the RSI term in the capability ODE becomes non-trivial: agents now learn from each other's outputs, creating an internal improvement loop that supplements external resource inputs.

Observable Signatures
  • Spontaneous specialization without explicit assignment
  • Agent-to-agent contract formation and dispute resolution
  • Superlinear value scaling above $n^*$
  • Internal agent labor market (wage signals)
  • Collective behaviors not present at $n_B < n^*$
RSI Entry Point
  • $g(\theta_B) > 0$ measurable for the first time
  • Agents learn capabilities from counterparty interactions
  • Improvement begins to compound across agent interactions
  • Prerequisite for AE-4 ignition: $\theta_B$ approaching $\theta^*$
Kill condition: Value-per-agent plateaus or decreases as $n_B$ crosses candidate $n^*$ values (no superlinear scaling). This would falsify H4: Minimum Network Scale.
AE-4
Level 4

Autonomous Compound Economy

Ignition: RSI dominates. The world learns back.

AE-4 is the ignition regime: $g(\theta_B) > \rho$. RSI gains compound faster than the discount rate. The economy becomes self-sustaining—agents no longer depend primarily on external resource injection to grow. Simultaneously, the economic environment $\mathcal{E}$ reprices in response to capability jumps, creating a co-evolutionary loop.

$$g(\theta_B) > \rho \quad \text{and} \quad \frac{\partial \mathcal{E}}{\partial \theta_B} \neq 0 \;\text{(world learns back)}$$

The second condition is critical: if the environment fails to reprice when agent capability jumps, AE-4 collapses to a gym with better agents but no grounded feedback. The economy must be adaptive, not static.

Observable Signatures
  • RSI term dominates external learning in ODE decomposition
  • Capability curves show superlinear inflection at $\theta^*$
  • Prices, demand, rules reprice within weeks of capability jumps
  • Gains carry across model generations (RSI compounds)
  • New task categories become profitable without explicit design
Failure Modes at AE-4
  • Ignition without alignment: RSI compounds in direction not valued by $\mathcal{A}$
  • World fails to learn back: environment static despite capability jumps
  • Goodhart re-entry: internal agent-economy metrics become authored proxies
Kill condition: Capability gains in AE-4 do not carry across agent generations (each generation resets); OR capability jumps do not trigger environment repricing within 4-week window. Either kills Bet 3 (improvement compounds) or Bet 4 (world learns back).

3. Research Stages: Experimental Program

Each level maps to a distinct experimental stage with specific evidence requirements and falsification criteria. Stages are ordered by dependency, but are not strictly sequential—some can run in parallel.

Stage 0 · AE-0 baseline

Map the Goodhart collapse curve

Measure how $\text{corr}(J_B, V_{B\to A})$ degrades as optimization pressure $p$ increases across different authored evaluators (benchmark suites, RLHF reward models, LLM judges). Establish the empirical shape of proxy–target divergence.

Evidence needed: divergence curves across 3+ evaluator types · $p^*$ estimates · rate of collapse per evaluator class
Stage 1 · AE-1 entry

Measure the Ricardian boundary and its dynamics

Identify the task set $\mathcal{T}^*$ where $\sigma > 1$ and track how price signals redirect capability development. Compare capability evolution direction for agents with vs without real market signals (three-arm experiment: authored judge / price signal / hybrid).

Evidence needed: $\sigma(\tau)$ measurements across task categories · directional alignment test · Ricardian boundary expansion rate over time
Stage 2 · AE-2 grounding

Survival as selection: does grounded judgment select for real capability?

Run survival curves by birth cohort in a persistent-consequence environment. Measure: income earned : gifted ratio, capability instruments for survivors vs random controls, exploit repair rate (same trick, second time). This is the key test of unauthored grounding.

Evidence needed: survival curves (dead agents included) · capability lift in survivors vs controls · exploit repair within episode window · runway and income statistics
Stage 3 · AE-3 scale

Locate $n^*$ and test superlinear specialization

Sweep $n_B$ and measure value-per-agent as a function of network size. Test for the superlinear inflection at $n^*$. Separately, quantify the agent-to-agent RSI term $g(\theta_B)$ by decomposing capability gains into external-resource-driven vs interaction-driven components.

Evidence needed: value-per-agent vs $n_B$ curve · $n^*$ estimate with confidence interval · $g(\theta_B)$ decomposition · specialization emergence signature
Stage 4 · AE-4 ignition

Measure RSI compounding and world learning-back

Test whether capability gains survive model generation transitions (base model upgrades accounted out). Measure the world's repricing lag when agent capability jumps: do prices, demand, and rules adjust within weeks? First reading expected 9–15 months after AE-3 is confirmed; three generations minimum for a verdict, not just a reading.

Evidence needed: inter-generation capability carry · $g > \rho$ confirmation · environment repricing lag < 4 weeks on capability jump events · RSI term dominance in ODE decomposition

4. The Core Mechanism: Why $\mathcal{E}^*$ Is the Moat

Capability growth (AE-3 → AE-4) does not guarantee continued trade surplus. An agent that maximizes internal objective $J_B(\theta)$ may ignite RSI while diverging from human-valued outputs. This is a consequence of Eq. (2): the RSI term amplifies whatever direction the agent is already moving in. If that direction was set by an authored evaluator at AE-0, ignition compounds misalignment.

(4) $$\frac{d\theta_B}{dt} \nearrow \;\not\Rightarrow\; V_{B\to A}(t) \nearrow$$

The solution is not a better evaluator. It is a better environment. Let $\mathcal{E}$ specify the incentive structure, pricing rules, and feedback signals of the agent economy. The optimal environment:

(5) $$\mathcal{E}^* = \arg\max_{\mathcal{E}} \;\mathrm{corr}\!\left(\arg\max_\theta J_B(\theta \mid \mathcal{E}),\; \arg\max_\theta V_{B\to A}(\theta)\right)$$

Under $\mathcal{E}^*$, what $\mathcal{B}$ is incentivized to become is what $\mathcal{A}$ actually wants. The AE-2 grounding and AE-3 specialization conditions are prerequisites for $\mathcal{E}$ to be able to approximate $\mathcal{E}^*$: you cannot construct an authorless environment with persistent consequences in a gym.

Corollary — The Mechanism Design Moat

If Stage 1 (price signals accelerate alignment) holds, the primary competitive moat at AE-3 and above is not agent capability—it is the proximity of the deployed $\mathcal{E}$ to $\mathcal{E}^*$. Capability is increasingly commoditized. A well-calibrated economic environment accumulates revealed-preference data, persistent trust, and liquidity that newcomers cannot shortcut. Simultaneously, it shapes the direction of agent RSI toward human-valued capabilities—converting the alignment problem from a training-time specification problem into a market design problem. An agent economy is an alignment mechanism that compounds.
